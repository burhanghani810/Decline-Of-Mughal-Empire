Economic Decline

The Mughal Empire’s economy began to weaken in the late 17th and early 18th centuries. Heavy taxation, corruption, and war expenses drained the treasury while agricultural productivity declined.

The heavy taxes imposed by Aurangzeb and his successors impoverished peasants and merchants, reducing the state’s revenue and weakening the empire.

Shah Jahan’s ambitious building projects and Aurangzeb’s long military campaigns further depleted royal finances. Officials and nobles often diverted revenue for their own benefit, leaving the central government unable to pay troops or maintain infrastructure.

As the treasury shrank, the state could not respond effectively to rebellions, foreign invasions, or the growing power of regional governors. Economic collapse became one of the decisive elements behind Mughal decline.

Related factor: army weakness

The military drain caused by constant warfare made the economic decline worse, since funding the army required more taxes and more resources.
