Foreign Invasions

Foreign invasions exposed the Mughal Empire’s growing weakness at a time when its economy, military, and administration were already strained.

Nadir Shah (1739)

Nadir Shah’s invasion culminated in the sack of Delhi and the loss of immense treasure, including the Peacock Throne. The raid demonstrated that the Mughal army could no longer protect the capital, devastating the empire’s reputation and finances.

Ahmad Shah Abdali (1761)

Ahmad Shah Abdali’s victory at the Third Battle of Panipat further weakened the Mughals and paved the way for regional powers to assert independence over northern India.

Repeated foreign attacks shattered Mughal prestige and left the empire vulnerable to internal rebellion and regional fragmentation.

These invasions occurred when the Mughal state was already fractured by court intrigue, weakened army cohesion, and financial strain. The defeats accelerated the empire’s decline and helped other powers gain control of former Mughal territories.
