Growth of Regional Power in the Mughal Empire:

Emergence of Powerful Regional Rulers:

The Mughal Empire saw the emergence of powerful regional rulers such as the Marathas, Rajputs, and Sikhs.

Control of Significant Territories:

These regional powers often controlled significant territories and had their own armies and administrative systems. Autonomy from Central Authority: Regional rulers maintained a degree of autonomy from the central Mughal authority, allowing them to govern their regions effectively.

Challenge to Central Authority:

The rise of regional power centers challenged the authority of the Mughal emperors and contributed to the fragmentation of the empire.

Decentralization in the Mughal Empire:

Shift of Power to Regional Governors:

Decentralization in the Mughal Empire refers to the shift of power from the central authority to regional governors, known as Subahdars, Nawabs, and Zamindars.

Increased Autonomy for Local Rulers:

These local rulers gained more autonomy in administering their territories, collecting revenue, and maintaining law and order.

Factors Leading to Decentralization:

The vast size of the empire and the difficulty of centralized control were factors that led to decentralization.

Factors Leading to Decentralization:

The vast size of the empire and the difficulty of centralized control were factors that led to decentralization. Decentralization weakened the Mughal Empire by diluting central authority and making it more susceptible to internal conflicts and external invasions.
